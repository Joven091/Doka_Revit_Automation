# Slab loads carried by beam formwork

Select **D3 Shoring System** and **Transfer slab loads through beam supports**. Enter the upper support spacing along the beam. The beam concrete is present before the slab loads arrive. Upper supports represent vertical load-transfer feet, without D3 geometry or independent supports to the floor.

The left/right inputs describe rectangular bays between matching parallel beam supports. Select **Individual beams and slab bays** for a chain of actual parallel beams: each beam has its own width and depth, and each connecting slab has its own clear width and thickness. **Add slab + beam** extends the chain; **Remove last bay** shortens it. This version supports 2–8 beams with a common longitudinal length. Different longitudinal lengths, skew bays and upper stock joints are outside this model. Saved calculations without transfer fields keep their original behavior.

## Load path

1. Double-H20 upper bearers receive the area load over their actual uncut stock length and each row's tributary width. Upper stocks leave 200–350 mm between their ends and the concrete beam faces. Foot centres must be at least 250 mm from a face and 150 mm from stock ends. Foot locations are optimized independently on each side of a network bay, with at least 300 mm between feet; this is a search bound, not a D3 dimension.
2. The slab strips between each concrete face and upper stock end are loaded separately through their adjacent beam's side shutter. Each strip's resultant acts **100 mm outward from the concrete face, toward the slab bay**. For example, a 300 mm strip contributes area load × 0.300 m × row tributary width. Upper coverage and the two strips together carry the full clear-bay slab load; they do not overlap. Slab above the concrete beam width is not counted again. Shutter strength and its connection are not verified.
3. A double-H20 lower transverse bearer receives the beam's own concrete load, the upper-foot reaction and each shutter reaction at its actual transverse position. Its stock and position are searched, including asymmetric arrangements. Both primary bearings and loaded feet require at least 150 mm of stock beyond their centres; bearing contact details remain separate.
4. The two lower-bearer reactions become point loads on the longitudinal double-H20 primary pairs. Each independent uncut primary stock has at least two supporting leg planes, with no overlap or continuity across member breaks. Transverse rows remain at least 40 mm from those breaks as a search allowance; actual widths and end details require verification.
5. Each actual tower-leg reaction is checked against the entered permissible load. For every beam, beam-only, each adjacent slab individually and both adjacent slabs together are checked. These local cases cover all combinations of slab loading across the chain. Negative reactions at any foot, bearing or leg invalidate a candidate. Leg-load envelopes are reported; their sum is not a simultaneous applied load.

All H20 members in this transfer model are pairs sharing load equally: moment 10 kNm, shear 22 kN, EI 899.7512 kNm². Relative member deflection uses the selected L/500, L/350 or L/270 criterion for each support span and overhang. Transfer mode requires a ratio criterion; existing calculator modes retain their legacy fixed-mm options.

Along the concrete beam, the primary stock end allowance defines the loaded length, with the first/last row tributaries closing at those stock ends. Longitudinal concrete end overhangs remain excluded from the loaded area. The transverse slab-edge strips described above are included. Sheathing and slab secondary H20s are not analyzed by this mode; tributary area load is delivered directly to upper bearers and shutters.

## Optimization and drawings

The single receiving-beam mode ranks its tower legs first, then H20 length. The network ranks **all receiving-beam tower legs plus upper feet first, then total H20 length**. Shared upper stocks are counted once. Network beams share the upper row positions and primary coverage length; their primary stock combinations and lower towers can differ. A bounded chain search compares compatible upper-bay candidates, using each beam's actual loading. It is deterministic, but does not prove a global minimum over every possible arrangement. Upper rows follow the entered maximum spacing with closing strips and small phase adjustments. Manufactured lower D3 frame/cross dimensions, rotated towers, unbraced gaps and the standalone-600 exclusion are respected.

The horizontally scrolling plan shows tower locations, primary stock lengths, frame/cross dimensions, gaps and stock-end-to-leg offsets. Lower transverse pairs are omitted from the plan; their spacing is stated. The section shows their full stock lengths, individual concrete depths, slab bays, upper stocks and foot clearances. Red arrows show upper-foot transfer and blue arrows show shutter-edge transfer. Network transverse bay widths and vertical geometry are schematic; dimension labels govern.

Press **Show shear, moment & deflection**, then select a primary pair directly from the main plan. Select a lower transverse or upper pair from the main section; it represents the identified maximum-tributary row. Selecting a member from another network beam switches to that beam's actual analysis. Load-case buttons show the lower-member stages, with point-load values/locations and exact shear jumps. Every diagram describes an H20 pair.

Estimated slab soffit movement includes upper flexure, lower transverse flexure and interpolated/extrapolated displacement of the two longitudinal primary lines at each foot. Network bays use the actual movements of both adjacent beams; single-beam mode mirrors the opposite beam. These two-support transverse and upper bearers are statically determinate, so differential support movements add an affine displacement without changing reactions. Displacement extrema are calculated from the beam polynomial. Combined movement is reported separately from relative member criteria; no total slab-movement tolerance is assumed.

## Verification boundary

This is a vertical force-transfer and H20 member calculation. Upper-support capacity/stability, shutter capacity/connections, baseplates, local bearing/splitting, equal-load-sharing hardware, sheathing, slab secondary timbers, tower height/bracing/stability and foundations are outside it. Numeric checks do not approve the concentrated-load bearing detail. [Doka timber-beam instructions](https://direct.doka.com/_ext/downloads/downloadcenter/999791002_2024_12_online.pdf) restrict heavy concentrated loads combined with wide spans. Pair properties use the [H20 top technical data](https://direct.doka.com/_ext/downloads/downloadcenter/999642202_2023_01_online.pdf).

Tests check force/moment equilibrium, full clear-bay load conservation including shutters, shared-bay reactions, individual beam depths, leg capacity, supported stock coverage, load stages and exact displacement/shear behavior. WPF checks cover adding/removing bays, individual-depth validation, plan/section selection, saved configurations, reports and legacy calculator behavior.
