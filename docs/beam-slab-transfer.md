# Slab loads carried by beam formwork

## Drawing workspace

Choose **Open drawing workspace** in the D3 beam calculator. A separate maximized WPF window shares the calculator's inputs and optimizer. Reopening brings the same window forward. Closing retains edits; changing the support system or closing the calculator closes the drawing window and releases its validation state. Opening a mirrored single-beam transfer setup converts its bays to matching actual beams; recalculate to obtain a network result.

The **Geometry** plan is drawn in one millimetre coordinate system before calculation. Select a beam to edit width/depth, or a slab to edit clear span/thickness, in the right panel. Click a slab-span or overall-length dimension to focus its exact numeric input. **Bay above/below** inserts a slab and matching beam beside the selected beam. **Crossing beam** arms placement: click its slab bay, then edit position, width and total depth. Move a main beam to adjust the preceding clear bay, or move a crossing beam along the common length; drag edits snap to 25 mm. **Remove** deletes the selected crossing beam, or a main beam together with its preceding slab bay (the first bay for the first beam). At least two network beams remain. Insertion/removal reindexes surviving crossing beams with their slab bays. Undo/redo restores geometry and settings, leaving results to be recalculated. Ctrl+Z/Y acts on drawing edits outside text fields; text fields retain their normal text undo.

**Calculate** uses the existing member, stage and leg checks. A successful result switches to **Formwork**, showing every tower leg and primary pair over the geometry. Fit shows the complete arrangement; wheel/toolbar controls zoom, and middle-button dragging pans. Double-click fits the active view. Fonts remain readable while plan geometry zooms. The selected beam shows support spans, stock-end offsets, frame/cross orientation and peak leg load; **All dimensions** exposes those details for the other beams. Red tower sides are cross braces, grey sides are frames; grey dimensions also mark free gaps. Hover a leg to read its reaction and permissible load. Primary stock lengths remain beside the primary lines.

Select a primary pair directly in plan to open shear, moment and deflection in the collapsible panel below the drawing. **Section** separately shows single slab/lower secondary H20 lengths, foot clearances and shutter transfer. Select a secondary there for its actual single-member diagrams. Primary diagrams show the governing UDL design model directly. The section has its own fit/zoom/pan camera; its transverse/vertical representation remains schematic. Stage buttons retain the exact existing diagram load cases.

Edits and conversion errors clear stale diagrams and support results in both windows. Calculate/export cannot use unfinished or invalid input. Failed calculations show copyable diagnostic details, with buttons that select the affected beam or slab on the geometry plan. A failed tower arrangement is never presented as a solution. **Save** can retain valid input geometry before calculation or after a no-solution result, using the existing Beam file format; **Export** requires a passing calculation. Loaded files retain existing validation and compatibility checks.

The drawing editor follows the existing solver: a single beam or a chain of 2–15 parallel main beams with a common length, rectangular slab bays and perpendicular crossing beams. It does not introduce arbitrary polygons, independent main-beam lengths, openings or angled beams, and does not change structural acceptance criteria or optimization ranking.

Select **D3 Shoring System** and **Transfer slab loads through beam supports**. Select the plywood product; the calculated maximum secondary spacing along the beam is shown in a locked field. The beam concrete is present before the slab loads arrive. Upper supports represent vertical load-transfer feet, without D3 geometry or independent supports to the floor.

The left/right inputs describe rectangular bays between matching parallel beam supports. Select **Individual beams and slab bays** for a chain of actual parallel beams: each beam has its own editable width and depth (separately labelled input columns), and each connecting slab has its own clear width and thickness. **Add slab + beam** extends the chain; **Remove last bay** shortens it. This version supports 2–15 beams with a common longitudinal length. Different longitudinal lengths, skew bays and upper stock joints are outside this model. Saved calculations without transfer fields keep their original behavior.

## Repeated symmetric slab supports

Slab H20 support feet must have **equal setbacks from the two adjacent concrete beam faces**, with centred stock and equal end overhangs. The same stock and setback repeat at every slab H20 row in that bay. The network search compares only symmetric arrangements, including bays between main beams of different widths or depths; a fixed asymmetric plan is rejected. The single-beam transfer search already uses symmetric feet. Schedules, sections and the workspace slab summary give the equal setback and maximum longitudinal row spacing for site setting out.

Uniform slab loading gives equal reactions at the two slab-H20 feet. It does not imply equal loads on receiving secondaries, primaries or tower legs. One-sided slab stages, different main-beam loads and cross beams retain their actual eccentric reactions and individual capacity checks and signed uplift reporting. Leg reactions are never averaged to make a layout pass. Primary UDL design and local cross-beam reaction footprints remain unchanged. If the constrained shared cross-beam arrangement fails, Automatic still evaluates dedicated towers.

The specified spacing, bearing positions and stock-end allowances require site control. Saved files retain their geometry, loads, elevations and crossing modes; recalculation now applies the symmetric slab support requirement, including in Legacy slab rows mode. Previously optimized support counts may change.

## Load path

1. Single H20 slab members receive the area load over their actual member length and at least the entered secondary spacing as tributary width, including end rows. Upper H20s leave at least 200 mm between their ends and the concrete beam faces. The maximum strip equals the permitted sheet spacing calculated from that slab’s area load, selected plywood and plywood deflection criterion; it is independent of any closer row-spacing retry. Fitting catalogue lengths are retained. In legacy single-beam mode, when none fits, use one custom length `floor((clear bay − 400) / 25) × 25` mm, rounded down so each end remains at least 200 mm from the beam face. This keeps edge strips closest to 200 mm and requires no upper joint. Custom lengths are labelled in the section and schedule; primary and lower secondary H20s still use the stock catalogue. Foot centres must be at least 250 mm from a face and 150 mm from member ends. One equal foot setback is selected for both sides and repeated at every row in a bay, with at least 300 mm between feet; this is a search bound, not a D3 dimension.
2. The slab strips between each concrete face and upper stock end are loaded separately through their adjacent beam's side shutter. Each strip's resultant acts **100 mm outward from the concrete face, toward the slab bay**. For example, a 300 mm strip contributes area load × 0.300 m × row tributary width. Upper coverage and the two strips together carry the full clear-bay slab load; they do not overlap. Slab above the concrete beam width is not counted again. Shutter strength and its connection are not verified.
3. A single-H20 lower transverse secondary receives the beam's own concrete load, the upper-foot reaction and each shutter reaction at its actual transverse position. Its stock is centred on the concrete beam; the slab-foot setbacks remain equal at both faces of each bay. Both primary bearings and loaded feet require at least 150 mm of stock beyond their centres; bearing contact details remain separate.
4. The lower-bearer reactions per metre define distributed loads on the longitudinal double-H20 primary pairs, independent of secondary row positions. Main-primary beam and slab UDL intensities remain unadjusted over their intended covered regions. Uncovered longitudinal end-strip loads are assigned to external near/far column-side supports; their capacity requires separate verification. Each construction stage reports these strip loads by end and primary line. For 6.90 m concrete and 6.80 m primary coverage, the UDL is unchanged: no 6.90/6.80 multiplier is applied. Slab-secondary end-strip effects, dedicated crossing adjustments and crossing-junction loads remain included. Crossing increments remain distributed over their physical longitudinal footprints. Primary shear, moment, deflection, tower reactions and displayed diagrams all use these same distributed design loads; there is no secondary point-load mode. Each independent uncut primary stock has at least two supporting leg planes, with no overlap or continuity across member breaks. Transverse rows remain at least 40 mm from those breaks as a search allowance; actual widths and end details require verification.
5. Each actual tower-leg reaction is checked against the entered permissible load. For every beam, beam-only, each adjacent slab individually and both adjacent slabs together are checked. These local cases cover all combinations of slab loading across the chain. Negative reactions at any foot, bearing or leg invalidate a candidate. Leg-load envelopes are reported; their sum is not a simultaneous applied load.

The H20 directly below the slab is single: moment 5 kNm, shear 11 kN, EI 449.8756 kNm². The lower transverse secondary uses the same single-member properties. Only longitudinal primary members are pairs sharing load equally: moment 10 kNm, shear 22 kN, EI 899.7512 kNm². Relative member deflection uses the selected L/500, L/350 or L/270 criterion for each support span and overhang. Transfer mode requires a ratio criterion; existing calculator modes retain their legacy fixed-mm options.

In network mode, the complete physical concrete length is accounted for. The main-primary UDL covers its intended stock region without increasing its intensity. Uncovered longitudinal end strips are assigned to external column-side supports and reported separately. Slab-secondary end strips retain signed two-row influence reactions preserving force and moment. Local beam-only secondary rows can reduce end demands while shared slab rows, foot locations and slab tributaries remain fixed. Single receiving-beam mode retains its existing stock-coverage model. The transverse slab-edge strips described above are included. The selected plywood chart sets the maximum secondary spacing; tributary area load is delivered directly to the single slab H20s and shutters.

## Optimization and drawings

Transfer/network searches compare a relative economic score: material length plus **5,000 mm of timber equivalent per tower leg** and **500 mm per upper foot**. These are provisional design preferences, not supplier prices or a complete bill of steel components; two saved tower legs justify five additional metres of timber. Historical replay uses brace preferences and stock/support symmetry to break economic ties. Production ranks leg count, grouping, brace preference and symmetry before material economy. Shared upper stocks are counted once. Network beams share the upper row positions and slab coverage length. Each beam can independently choose primary stock coverage at least as long as the shared slab coverage, within the entered concrete end allowance. Extra beam-concrete load is carried by checked local beam-only rows and/or end rows; these add no upper feet and slab tributaries remain shared. Primary stock combinations and lower towers can differ. Mirrored stock families and mirrored regular tower groups survive independent candidate caps. A bounded chain search compares compatible upper-bay candidates using each beam's actual loading, and modern crossings receive a coordinated improvement pass even after a passing plan is found. It is deterministic, but does not prove a global minimum over every possible arrangement. Upper rows follow the entered maximum spacing with closing strips and small phase adjustments. Manufactured lower D3 frame/cross dimensions, rotated towers, unbraced gaps and the standalone-600 exclusion are respected.

The horizontally scrolling plan shows tower locations, each primary stock length once, frame/cross dimensions, gaps and stock-end-to-leg offsets. Braced bays and their dimension lines are red; free gaps and their dimensions are grey. No C/G prefixes are used. Primary lengths sit beside the primary line and all stock-end offsets share a baseline. Five connected frame planes can split into a two-frame standalone tower and a three-frame joined tower, removing one cross bay without removing any leg; each resulting tower must still meet the standalone-600 restriction. The legend identifies paired primaries, single secondaries and their spacing; all dimensions are in mm. Each beam header shows its width × depth, and each slab bay shows its clear width and thickness. Lower transverse secondaries are omitted from the plan; their spacing is stated. The section shows their full stock lengths, individual concrete depths, slab bays, upper stocks and foot clearances. Red arrows show upper-foot transfer and blue arrows show shutter-edge transfer. Network transverse bay widths and vertical geometry are schematic; dimension labels govern.

Press **Show shear, moment & deflection**, then select a primary pair directly from the main plan. Select a single lower transverse or slab H20 from the main section; it represents the governing loaded row, including crossing-beam loads where present. Selecting a member from another network beam switches to that beam's actual analysis. Load-case buttons show the lower-member stages, with point-load values/locations and exact shear jumps. Diagram titles and permissible shear/moment lines use the selected member’s single/pair count.

Estimated slab soffit movement includes upper flexure, lower transverse flexure and interpolated/extrapolated displacement of the two longitudinal primary lines at each foot. Network bays use the actual movements of both adjacent beams; single-beam mode mirrors the opposite beam. These two-support transverse and upper bearers are statically determinate, so differential support movements add an affine displacement without changing reactions. Displacement extrema are calculated from the beam polynomial. Combined movement is reported separately from relative member criteria; no total slab-movement tolerance is assumed.

## Legacy crossing beams and load inputs

In **Individual beams and slab bays**, expand **Crossing beams** and add a row. Enter the slab bay number, centre distance from the longitudinal beam start, crossing beam width and total depth (including slab thickness). Each perpendicular beam spans the selected clear slab bay and loads the existing single slab H20s; it has no separate floor supports. Multiple crossing beams may occupy a bay, but their footprints cannot overlap. Removing a bay also removes its crossing beams. Inputs and the crossing-load schedule are saved and exported.

For slab or beam total thickness/depth `h` in metres, the default area load is `q(h) = 25h + clamp(0.10 × 25h, 0.75, 1.75) + 0.75 + 0.40 kN/m²`. In the existing code, the capped 10% term is named heaping/placing surcharge, the separate 0.75 is working load and 0.40 is formwork dead load. A 300 mm slab gives 9.40 kN/m²; a 1,750 mm beam gives 46.65 kN/m² before multiplication by its width. Custom loads replace these three allowances with the entered live and formwork values; the default surcharge is not added again in that mode.

The incremental crossing load is `[q(crossing total depth) − q(slab thickness)] × crossing width × clear bay width`. This counts existing slab concrete and its allowances once. Baseline slab tributaries remain unchanged; the incremental load is assigned only to H20 rows physically beneath the crossing footprint, using midpoint tributaries between those rows and closing strips at the beam faces. A footprint with no H20 row below it is invalid. The complete crossing footprint must lie within longitudinal primary coverage. Across the bay, the stock-covered portion loads the H20, and both uncovered edge portions use the established shutter load path at 100 mm outward. Each row's actual reactions are used to check the lower single H20. Primary pairs and tower legs use distributed line loads; crossing increments remain local to each crossing footprint rather than averaged along the whole beam. Crossing concrete is included with that bay's slab-loaded case, after main beam concrete is present.

The plan identifies X1, X2, etc. with width × depth and centre position. The existing section is schematic; crossing-beam soffit levels, bearing details and the local formwork needed to deliver load onto the existing H20s are not designed by this model. A heavier crossing beam may require closer upper rows or a different load-transfer arrangement if no candidate passes.

## Verification boundary

This is a vertical force-transfer and H20 member calculation. Upper-support capacity/stability, shutter capacity/connections, baseplates, local bearing/splitting, equal-load-sharing hardware, plywood orientation/joints/end details, tower height/bracing/stability and foundations are outside it. Numeric checks do not approve the concentrated-load bearing detail. [Doka timber-beam instructions](https://direct.doka.com/_ext/downloads/downloadcenter/999791002_2024_12_online.pdf) restrict heavy concentrated loads combined with wide spans. Pair properties use the [H20 top technical data](https://direct.doka.com/_ext/downloads/downloadcenter/999642202_2023_01_online.pdf).

Tests check force/moment equilibrium, full clear-bay load conservation including shutters, shared-bay reactions, individual beam depths, leg capacity, supported stock coverage, load stages and exact displacement/shear behavior. WPF checks cover adding/removing bays, individual-depth validation, plan/section selection, saved configurations, reports and legacy calculator behavior.

## Diagnosing a failed search

Searches reuse transverse options within a receiving-beam solve, checked lower row cases across primary layouts, and tower support geometries within each catalogue/custom pass. Caches are discarded between passes and runs; row keys include support positions, tributary widths and effective crossing loads. This preserves the candidate order, ranking and engineering limits. The diagnostic report includes tower geometry builds/cache hits, transverse option cache hits and lower row-case cache hits. Numerical counters count checks actually performed; reused passing or failing checks are not counted again. `tests/Beam.Tests` supports `--search-reuse` for cached/uncached equivalence checks and `--reported-benchmark` for the 10,600 mm three-beam case at 275 mm row spacing. The benchmark's three-minute cancellation limit applies only to the CI test, not the calculator.

When optimization cannot find a valid result, **Calculation failure details** appears below the warning. Expand it to read or copy the report. Stock/edge coverage and support geometry are reported separately from numeric checks. Member failures show the beam/slab identifier, available member/row/load-case location, calculated demand, permissible value and a suggested input or layout to review. Checks include upper/lower single-H20 and primary-pair moment, shear and governing-span/overhang deflection, negative bearing reactions and individual tower-leg load. Crossing footprints outside primary coverage or between support rows, missing stock lengths and row-to-primary-joint conflicts also have explicit messages. No failed layout is shown as a solution; editing inputs, restoring a calculation or a successful retry clears stale diagnostics.

The report retains the closest recorded failing sample for each check, with failure counts. Samples can come from different candidate layouts and are not a proposed alternative. These are bounded-search observations, not proof of infeasibility or approval to relax a criterion.

Example: a 2,575 mm clear bay now uses a **2,175 mm custom single slab H20**, with 200 mm edge strips at both beam faces. The load over that member and both shutter strips still sums to the full clear-bay slab load.

Under the former primary point-load model, for the reported 10,600 mm setup (800×1,750 and 650×1,150 mm main beams, 300 mm slab, 70 kN legs and standalone-600 exclusion), a reproduction using the default **750 mm upper-row spacing** reaches structural checks but fails the lower single H20 moment, shear and deflection checks, including the beam-only stage. Searches at 500, 350 and 275 mm also do not find a complete passing layout. At **200 mm spacing**, the bounded search finds a passing layout with 36 tower legs (24 under B1 and 12 under B2), retaining the 2,175 mm custom slab members. This is an example for those entered settings, not a global minimum or an automatic spacing change. Failure samples may belong to different candidates and cannot be combined into one arrangement.


## Primary UDL design model

Longitudinal primary design does not rely on exact secondary stations. For each primary line and construction stage, the transverse beam reaction per metre and each active slab reaction per metre define the UDL. Full beam/slab reaction per metre is applied over each complete primary stock, with no end-row or slab-coverage-end reduction. The entered spacing is the maximum permitted secondary spacing. Upper and lower secondary design loads also retain full tributaries at end rows; covered-area totals remain physical reference quantities and can be lower than the design loads. Crossing-beam increments are distributed over their actual footprints, retaining local load peaks and total load. Lower-secondary and slab member checks retain their physical transverse load positions and use at least the entered maximum row spacing as tributary width. The primary numerical checks, stock demands, tower reactions, diagrams, schedules and diagnostics all use this distributed model; the former equivalent-UDL approximation and primary point-load switch are removed.

The photographed 2,475 mm stock with a 1,524 mm support span and 31.572 kN/m UDL develops 24.058 kN shear, exceeding the double-H20 22 kN limit. The solver must reject that UDL arrangement. Historical economy counts obtained with the removed primary point-load model are not acceptance targets.


## Dedicated cross-beam formwork

New cross beams default to **Automatic**. **Existing supports only** requires a passing arrangement on the main beams' transverse receiving secondaries. **Dedicated towers** designs primary pairs, single secondary H20s and independent tower legs for the cross beam. Automatic tries shared bearings first and falls back to towers when geometry, stock, member or leg checks fail. A failed requested arrangement is reported with the bounded-search diagnostics.

The main beam and cross beam bottom/soffit elevations are editable in both calculator and drawing workspace, with positive z upwards. Their initial values are minus concrete depth, so the initial concrete tops align at zero. H20 solids use 200 mm depth and 80 mm flange width. Checks include full catalogue/custom stock extensions, primary pairs, receiving secondaries, slab H20s, cross H20s, filler blocks and tower leg columns. Surface contact is permitted; interior overlap is rejected. Tower braces and hardware are not represented. Main-beam receiving stock end allowances and cross-stock bearing/end allowances are at least 150 mm. Shared cross stock cannot extend into main concrete. Dedicated primary coverage is complete across the clear bay; no cross load is discarded at stock ends.

Each shared bearing has its own filler height:

`cross H20 underside elevation - receiving secondary top elevation`

A zero gap permits direct bearing, a positive gap requires filler timber, and a negative gap invalidates shared support. The schedule reports each bearing location, reaction and height, and the drawing shows bearings/fillers at their elevations. **These heights are geometry only. Filler timber bearing strength, restraint and stability are not verified.** Sheathing, connections, H20 bearing, tower stability, braces and foundations require separate verification.

Cross formwork carries the clear-bay cross concrete and applicable construction/formwork loads. It receives no additional slab tributary. Its footprint is excluded from slab loads and slab H20 rows, including flange overlap at footprint edges; the remaining slab tributaries close around the excluded areas. Shared reactions load receiving secondaries at the actual bearing positions. Their primary-line reaction increments stay distributed over each cross row's actual longitudinal tributary footprint. The current primary UDL baseline and full-spacing main beam checks are retained. Physical load accounting and conservative design tributaries are reported separately.

Stages include main-beam concrete with each adjacent slab combination, independently combined with every subset of up to eight shared cross beams per receiving beam. Larger shared sets use conservative absolute response and signed reaction bounds covering every subset. Each stage checks moment, shear, relative member deflection and individual leg capacity, while reporting signed uplift. Support movement is reported as an estimate without a project tolerance. Search candidates and spacing alternatives are bounded; a global optimum is not established.

Cross supports contribute H20 pieces, timber length and tower legs to network quantities and ranking. Plan and section members open their actual applied loads and shear/moment/deflection diagrams. Saved network format 2 includes modes and bottom elevations. Older network XML is explicitly migrated to **Legacy slab rows**, preserving its previous slab-row crossing model; adding a crossing to an old document still defaults to Automatic.

Validation commands: `dotnet run --project tests/Beam.Tests/Beam.Tests.csproj -c Release` (or `-- --cross-supports` / `-- --primary-udl`), `dotnet build src/MyRevitAddin.csproj -c Release --warnaserror`, and `dotnet build tests/Beam.UiSmoke/Beam.UiSmoke.csproj -c Release --warnaserror`. On Windows, run the Beam.UiSmoke executable and Beam.Net48 `--cross-supports` regressions. `Microsoft.NETFramework.ReferenceAssemblies` remains in every net48 project, and this numerical/UI change introduces no Revit database calls or transactions.

## Uplift search policy

Uplift is excluded from candidate rejection for all beam/tower search inputs, including legacy callers assigning `IgnoreUplift=false`. Signed reactions and minimum leg reactions remain visible. Negative reactions require restraint; bearing-only supports cannot carry tension. A solution requiring tensile restraint is conditional on separate support/anchorage verification. Stabilizing deadweight and connection adequacy are not calculated. Every slab, lower-secondary, primary and crossing H20 must still pass moment, shear and relative deflection checks in all calculated construction stages. Maximum individual compression remains checked.


## Dedicated crossings with non-catalogue bay lengths

Dedicated towers respect the entered concrete end allowance instead of demanding exact stock coverage of the clear bay. Additional custom H20 lengths remain multiples of 25 mm. End strips are assigned by signed two-row sheathing statics to the outer two secondary rows at each end. Their influence reactions preserve full concrete force and moment; they are added as localized loads to the primary UDL baseline. The secondary design retains at least the full entered tributary spacing. End-strip loads participate in the candidate search, moment, shear, relative deflection, individual-leg checks and diagrams. Sheathing, bearing and connections still require separate design.

Dedicated searches own their diagnostic scope and retain the specific failure when cached. An unavailable cross arrangement leads the network failure summary. Diagnostic inputs include main/cross soffit elevations and requested crossing support modes.

The uploaded 7 October 2026 Automatic case (6,900 mm, eight alternating 800×1,750 and 650×1,150 beams, seven 650×1,150 crossings, 275 mm rows and 75 mm end allowance) now reaches a real geometry rejection: X1's dedicated primary H20 overlaps the B2 primary at the same elevation. That two-layer arrangement remains rejected; the fix does not establish a complete solution for this geometry. The former exact 2,677 mm stock rejection and unrelated 70.029 kN headline no longer mask the cross arrangement failure.

Run `--cross-end-strips` for full-load equilibrium, H20 checks, cache/reference comparisons, non-25 mm bays, zero-allowance rejection and diagnostic coverage. Run `--cross-reported` to verify the uploaded Automatic case retains its physical clash rejection. These checks also run in Windows CI, with end-strip regressions repeated under .NET Framework 4.8.

## Designer preferences and centred secondary H20s

At equal support count, beam and network searches avoid standalone 600 mm towers, minimize braced-bay count, prefer wider standalone cross braces, then minimize member quantity/length. Linked groups receive no standalone-width penalty; redundant-connection removal retains the fewest existing links needed to avoid narrow standalone groups when possible. Rotated towers use the actual cross dimension, rather than the 1524 mm frame span. Smaller standalone braces remain available when required for a feasible layout. Support/brace counts are cost proxies, not catalogue prices or a height-dependent stability check. The same comparison applies to search bounds, catalogue/custom selection, receiver-chain states and final results, including dedicated crossing towers.

Historical replay centres each lower secondary H20 on its concrete beam. Production also considers centring the stock on the primary bearing pair when tower legs are offset, preferring balanced cantilevers if every actual load and bearing retains its required end allowance. Member analysis and drawings use the selected physical coordinates. Plan H20 colours distinguish orange paired primaries from purple single secondaries; the existing secondary-visibility toggle and member selection remain available. Diagnostics include tower preferences and secondary midpoint offsets.

`dotnet run --project tests/Beam.Tests -c Release -- --designer-preferences` checks wide/narrow/linked/rotated tower choices, mirrored one-sided secondary centring and the diagnostic's three parallel-beam dimensions.

### Search duration and diagnostics

Normal Calculate/Optimize has no elapsed-time deadline. The 30-second solution time is a performance target to reach through optimization, rather than a stopping rule. Catalogue, custom-length and separate-slab fallback passes run to completion within the existing candidate limits. Users can cancel a running calculation; cancelled results are discarded. Only fully checked complete layouts are applied.

Diagnostics record the actual worker count, the absence of a normal search deadline, time to the first complete network candidate, complete-network candidate count, and times for stock covers, upper options, support geometry, receiving-beam searches, transverse/primary numerical analyses, movement and result application. Interactive diagnostics additionally record input validation, solver wall time and drawing preparation. Per-member numerical timings are collected in fast searches; unrestricted full searches retain enclosing stage timings and numerical counters to avoid per-member timing allocation overhead on .NET Framework. Nested stages and summed worker times overlap; they must not be added to obtain elapsed wall time. Drawing preparation measures synchronous refresh/setup, not asynchronous WPF painting. Timings include completed calls, including interrupted calls whose timing scopes have unwound. The last completed full-search trace is retained separately so a later alternatives operation cannot erase it. Starting a new session clears both traces.

Catalogue stock-cover generation visits only totals reachable on the catalogue's greatest-common-divisor grid and sorts stock lengths once. It retains the previous cover caps, ordering, structural model and ranking. Exact numerical response caches and existing pruning remain enabled. Timed modern networks first try one closer permitted sheet spacing and coverage near the middle of the allowed range. Dedicated crossing checks try the fully checked junction arrangement first. Normal unrestricted calculation and reference traversal keep the existing candidate order.

Run `dotnet run --project tests/Beam.Tests/Beam.Tests.csproj -c Release -- --search-budget` for unrestricted-default, opt-in diagnostic deadline, cancellation, retained-result and diagnostic-history checks. Run with `--diagnostic-full-benchmark` to measure the exact three-beam/two-bay/two-crossing inputs uploaded on 2026-10-14 without an elapsed-time limit; it records first complete candidate and total solver time and verifies the returned network, legs and members. Run with `--diagnostic-budget-benchmark` to reproduce the 6,900 mm, four-beam/three-bay geometry from the uploaded 2026-10-13 diagnostic. The benchmark follows the interactive plywood-spacing configuration and includes dedicated crossing supports and SlabSupports-only custom-length scope. Console timings exclude WPF painting and do not prove deployed Revit latency. Existing direct solver benchmarks omit the interactive budget unless they explicitly supply a timed trace, allowing exhaustive bounded-search comparisons.

## Local crossing-support coordination

After selecting a checked network, a single-run, four-leg dedicated crossing tower can be centred on its existing primary stock. Its primary response and individual leg reactions are checked again at the new support positions. A conflicting beam-only receiving H20 can move along the main beam; another H20 is added only if needed to keep every physical gap within the original design spacing. Full stock, leg, frame and concrete clearances are checked before the adjusted arrangement is accepted.

Plywood sheet-joint rows, primary-stock end rows, slab-foot rows and crossing-end-zone bearings stay fixed. A change blocked by one of these components retains the original checked arrangement. Main-primary UDLs and conservative secondary design loads retain their original spacing basis; physical positions and extra H20 quantities appear in the plan and schedule. Moving or adding an H20 does not introduce another concrete tributary into the primary UDL.

Selected horizontal dimension labels use clear lanes above their dimension lines, including when short setting-out segments require a leader.

### Crossing-junction work reuse

Junction placement builds a read-only spatial index of its fixed obstacle snapshot.
